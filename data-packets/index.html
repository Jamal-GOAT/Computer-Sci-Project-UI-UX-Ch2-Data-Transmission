How Data Travels

A packet goes through several key stages.

1.📱

Data is Created

When we send a message, image, video, or requests, our device converts the information into digital datas that can be sent through a network

2.📦

Data Is Split Into Packets

Since the amount of data can be big, it is divided into smaller pieces called packets. Each packet contains part of the original data along the information needed to help it reach its destination.

3.📡

Packets Travel Through Routers

Packets travel across the network through routers. Routers examine each packet's and decide where to forward it. Different packets can take different routes, depending on which routers are the fastest currently.

4.🔗

Data Is Reassembled

When the packets reach the destination, the receiving device puts the back together in the correct order. The original data can then be displayed or used by the receiver.

Data Packet

Header

⬇

The header contains information about the packet, such as its source and destination addresses. It helps routers determine where the packet needs to go and allows the receiver to identify and correctly process the packet.

Payload

⬇

The payload contains the actual data being transmitted, such as part of a message, image, or file. Large data is divided into smaller packets, with each packet carrying a portion of the original data in its payload.

Trailer

⬇

The trailer marks the end of the packet and contains error-checking information. It helps the receiver detect whether the packet was damaged during transmission.
